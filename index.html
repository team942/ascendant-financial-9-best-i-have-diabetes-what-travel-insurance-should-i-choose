Never write "you do not have to repay it". Write that the schedule is the owner's to set and the loan is still a real debt. INTEREST. Repaying restores the room used. It does not double the limit. CONSEQUENCES OF NOT REPAYING. Interest compounds; the death benefit shrinks by the loan and interest; the policy can lapse if loan plus interest outgrows cash value; a lapse or surrender with a loan outstanding can create a taxable amount in Canada; an outstanding loan reduces future borrowing room. Three questions precede every loan: what will this money do, named specifically; where will the repayment come from, named as a real source; what happens if the plan does not work. The rule that never changes: do not spend more than you make. A policy loan gives control, not extra income. CANADIAN POLICY-LOAN TAX, needs a tax adviser to sign off before publishing. A policy loan is itself a disposition of an interest under subsection 148(9), so a taxable policy gain can arise when the loan is taken, not only on lapse or surrender. Proceeds of disposition on a loan are the lesser of the loan amount excluding any portion used to pay a premium, and the cash surrender value immediately before the loan minus outstanding loan balances. The gain is those proceeds minus the adjusted cost basis immediately before disposition, included under subsection 148(1). Loan repayments are added back to the adjusted cost basis, capped at the loan's proceeds of disposition, so a repaid loan generally neutralises the gain. Never present a Canadian policy loan as automatically tax free, and never state a flat rule that loans above adjusted cost basis are taxable without the repayment-restores-basis point. A policy loan is itself a disposition of an interest under subsection 148(9), so a taxable policy gain can arise when the loan is taken, not only on lapse or surrender. Proceeds of disposition on a loan are the lesser of the loan amount excluding any portion used to pay a premium, and the cash surrender value immediately before the loan minus outstanding loan balances. The gain is those proceeds minus the adjusted cost basis immediately before disposition, included under subsection 148(1). Loan repayments are added back to the adjusted cost basis, capped at the loan's proceeds of disposition, so a repaid loan generally neutralises the gain. Never present a Canadian policy loan as automatically tax free, and never state a flat rule that loans above adjusted cost basis are taxable without the repayment-restores-basis point.
